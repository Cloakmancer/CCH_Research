Literature roadmap — CCH, SPH & AI flood prediction

Organised as a progressive reading sequence. Click any card to prompt a deeper explanation.

Phase 1 Conceptual foundations — read first
Zscheischler et al. (2020) — Nature Reviews Earth & Environment CCH
The definitive typology of compound weather and climate events. Proposes four categories: preconditioned, multivariate, temporally compounding, and spatially compounding. Adopted by IPCC AR6.
Start here. This paper defines the vocabulary the entire field uses. Every subsequent paper cites it.
Salvadori & De Michele (2004) — Water Resources Research Copula / Stats
Foundational paper introducing copulas to hydrology. Derives joint return periods (AND/OR definitions) for bivariate events such as flood peak and volume. Theoretically rigorous but accessible.
The mathematical backbone. Read alongside a copula textbook chapter — Nelsen (2006) "Introduction to Copulas" is the standard reference.
Gómez-Gesteira et al. (2012) — Computers & Geosciences SPH
The SPHysics framework paper — describes the SPH governing equations, kernel functions, boundary conditions, and validation test cases. The theoretical foundation for DualSPHysics.
Required reading before touching DualSPHysics. Sets out the Lagrangian formulation in accessible engineering terms.
Phase 2 Core methodology — build the toolkit
Serinaldi & Kilsby (2015) — Advances in Water Resources Non-stationarity
Critical methodological paper arguing that non-stationarity adds model uncertainty that often exceeds sampling uncertainty. Challenges naive use of time-varying return periods. A necessary counterweight to over-confident non-stationary models.
Read this before claiming your model accounts for climate change — it will sharpen your thinking about what you can and cannot infer from short records.
Bevacqua et al. (2019) — Science Advances Compound Flood
Quantifies joint probability of storm surge and heavy precipitation across European coasts using vine copulas. Shows that climate change will increase compound flood probability substantially. A template for regional compound flood studies.
The go-to methodological template for a coastal compound flooding study using copulas. Code and data are publicly available.
Crespo et al. (2015) — Computer Physics Communications DualSPHysics
The main DualSPHysics code paper. Describes GPU-accelerated SPH, open-source implementation, and validation against dam-break and wave impact benchmarks. Essential for understanding the software you will use.
Read alongside the DualSPHysics user guide. This gives you the theory behind every parameter you will set.
Kratzert et al. (2018) — Hydrology & Earth System Sciences AI / LSTM
The landmark paper demonstrating that LSTM networks can match or outperform established conceptual rainfall-runoff models (SAC-SMA, HBV) without prior physical knowledge. Opened the door to data-driven hydrology at scale.
The paper that convinced the hydrology community that deep learning is serious. Read before implementing any LSTM flood model.
Phase 3 Applied modeling — get close to your research problem
Ward et al. (2018) — Environmental Research Letters Compound Flood
Global-scale analysis of dependence between storm surge and river discharge in delta and estuary settings. Directly relevant to South Asian river systems including Nepal's major rivers that drain into the Gangetic delta. Uses copula-based dependence metrics.
Particularly relevant given Nepal's context — major rivers (Koshi, Gandaki, Karnali) flow through delta systems where compound flooding is physically plausible.
Tan et al. (2022) — Acta Geotechnica / related debris flow SPH literature SPH Debris
Covers multiphase SPH-DEM coupling for debris flow simulations. Quantifies impact pressures on rigid and flexible retention structures. Validation against scaled flume experiments. Directly applicable to check dam design in mountain catchments.
Nepal sits in one of the world's most active debris flow zones. This thread connects SPH directly to your geographic context.
Raissi et al. (2019) — Journal of Computational Physics PINNs
The foundational PINNs paper. Shows how physical governing equations can be embedded as loss function terms in a neural network, constraining predictions to be physically consistent. Applied to fluid mechanics PDEs including Navier-Stokes.
If you pursue the AI + SPH physics integration angle, this paper is the theoretical bridge between them.
Sadegh et al. (2018) — Geophysical Research Letters Multi-hazard
Develops multi-hazard scenarios for compound extreme events using copula methods. Introduces the MvCAT toolbox (Multivariate Copula Analysis Toolbox) for R and MATLAB. Directly applicable to infrastructure fragility analysis under compound loading.
The MvCAT toolbox is a ready-to-use implementation of copula fitting — reduces coding overhead significantly for your first compound flood model.
Phase 4 Research frontier — define your contribution
Löwe et al. (2021) / Zhu et al. (2021) — Surrogate flood models Surrogate Models
Recent work on training deep learning models (CNN, LSTM, U-Net) directly on 2D hydrodynamic model outputs to produce real-time flood inundation maps. Demonstrates speed-ups of 100–1000× over numerical solvers with acceptable accuracy.
This is the methodological gap where SPH-generated training data + AI surrogate model is most publishable. Currently almost no work uses SPH specifically as the training data source.
AghaKouchak et al. (2020) — Annual Review of Earth & Planetary Sciences Climate Extremes
Comprehensive review of climate extremes and compound hazards in a warming world. Covers methodological challenges of data scarcity, non-stationarity, and the gap between scientific understanding and engineering practice in developing country contexts.
Sets up the research gap argument for your study — particularly strong for a Nepal/South Asia framing where data scarcity is a real constraint.
Domínguez et al. (2022) — Computer Physics Communications DualSPHysics v5
DualSPHysics v5.0 release paper. Documents new features including multi-phase flows, improved boundary conditions, coupling with external solvers, and enhanced GPU performance. The current state of the art for the platform you are using.
Read this alongside your DualSPHysics installation — it documents v5 capabilities that older tutorials don't cover.
Nevo et al. (2022) — Nature Operational AI
Documents Google's global flood forecasting system, which uses deep learning to issue operational flood alerts across 80+ countries including South Asia. Discusses transfer learning, data scarcity, and real-world deployment challenges.
The gold standard for what AI flood prediction looks like in operational practice. Grounding for any research aimed at real-world impact.
