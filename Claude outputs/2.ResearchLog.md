### Decisions

* Semiparametric (pseudo-ML) fitting adopted as the default over IFM — empirical ranks for
  margins, parametric copula — since margin choice for flood data (GEV vs LP3 vs GLO) is itself
  contested and misspecification there would contaminate θ̂.
* Copula selection will not be by AIC alone. Plan: empirical λ_U(u) plotted over u ∈ [0.9, 0.99]
  against candidate families, plus Cramér–von Mises with parametric bootstrap, and 2–3 families
  carried through to the design-value stage to report the spread.
* Gumbel retained as lead family for peak–volume, justified theoretically (only Archimedean that
  is max-stable / an extreme-value copula, consistent with annual-maxima margins) rather than
  only empirically.

### Correction to prior plan

`1.ProjectStatud.md` next-actions listed "compute AND and OR return periods" as one step. It is
not a single step: AND vs OR is a failure-mechanism question, not a statistical one. Peak-alone
OR volume-alone failure → OR; joint-coincidence failure → AND. T_AND ≥ T_OR always, so using AND
where the physics is OR yields an inflated return period and an undersized design. Also noted
that a bivariate T defines an isoline, not a unique design event — needs most-likely-design-event
or Kendall return period to resolve.

### Open Questions

* Which failure mechanism the eventual case study targets — this determines AND vs OR and should
  be fixed before design values are computed.
* Still unresolved from 2026-07-25: real dataset vs pseudo-observations as the resume point.

### Knowledge Gained

* The univariate skewness lesson is the one-dimensional shadow of the copula argument already
  made in the revision doc: a Normal *margin* understates single-variable extremes for the
  same structural reason a Gaussian *copula* (zero upper-tail dependence) understates joint
  extremes. Useful framing — the elementary track is not merely prerequisite, it rehearses
  the same failure mode one dimension down.
* Where the error bites: a Normal assumption biases $P(Q > q^*)$ low, and the bias *grows*
  with distance into the tail — i.e. it is largest exactly at the trigger thresholds CCH
  analysis exists to characterise.

### Decisions

* The statistics track gets its own top-level site section rather than being appended to the
  research log. Rationale: different register (introductory) and different cadence (near-daily
  vs per-session), so mixing them would make the research log harder to scan for actual
  methodology decisions. Cross-links carry the connection instead.
* Every stats entry must end with a CCH bridge. An entry without one is a note, not a log
  entry, and stays in `Stat_Study/`.

### Open Questions

* Skewness coefficient is descriptive only; quantifying tail weight needs the GEV shape
  parameter $\xi$. Deferred until the study track reaches distribution fitting — at which
  point the Nepal daily rainfall series in `Stat_Study/04_Resources/` is the obvious first
  real dataset, and may also settle the long-open "real data vs pseudo-observations" question
  from 2026-07-25.
* Mean−median gap as a tail diagnostic is confounded with sample size under heavy tails.
  Check its stability on a real record before relying on it.
