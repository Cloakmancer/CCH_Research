# Literature Reading Guide — Section-Level Focus

Companion to `CCH_literature_roadmap.html`. For each paper: what to read closely, what to skim
or skip, and why it matters to this project (CCH copula framework + SPH + AI, Nepal focus).

---

## Phase 1 — Conceptual foundations

**Zscheischler et al. (2020), *Nature Reviews Earth & Environment***
Read: Figure 1 (the four-class typology diagram) and Table 1 (event-type definitions).
Skip: the extended case-study catalog unless you need a citable precedent for a specific
event type. This paper is the vocabulary source, not a methods source.

**Salvadori & De Michele (2004), *Water Resources Research***
Read: the section deriving T_AND and T_OR (mid-paper) and the worked bivariate flood
example (peak–volume). These are the exact formulas your AND/OR return-period computation
is built on.
Skip: early formal copula axioms/Sklar's-theorem proofs unless you need the theorem stated
rigorously for a methods section.

**Gómez-Gesteira et al. (2012), *Computers & Geosciences***
Read: governing equations (SPH continuity/momentum formulation) and kernel function
definitions — needed before setting any DualSPHysics parameter.
Skip: the historical SPH literature review.

---

## Phase 2 — Core methodology

**Serinaldi & Kilsby (2015), *Advances in Water Resources***
Read: the argument section contrasting model uncertainty vs. sampling uncertainty. Gives you
defensible caveat language for any non-stationary return-period claim.
Skip: the full statistical test battery unless replicating their diagnostic directly.

**Bevacqua et al. (2019), *Science Advances***
Read: Methods (vine copula fitting to bivariate surge–precipitation data) and the joint
probability maps in Results — direct template for your compound-probability computation.
Note: their data/code availability statement is worth checking for a reusable pipeline.

**Crespo et al. (2015), *Computer Physics Communications***
Read: GPU implementation section and the validation benchmarks (dam-break, wave impact) —
these map directly onto your first DualSPHysics setup.
Skip: code architecture internals unless debugging the solver itself.

**Kratzert et al. (2018), *Hydrology & Earth System Sciences***
Read: LSTM architecture/formulation section and the benchmark comparison against
SAC-SMA/HBV in Results — sets your baseline performance target for any AI rainfall-runoff work.

---

## Phase 3 — Applied modeling

**Ward et al. (2018), *Environmental Research Letters***
Read: regional results for South/Southeast Asian deltas — look specifically for
Ganges–Brahmaputra basin figures, the closest geographic analog to Koshi/Gandaki/Karnali.
Skip: regions outside South/Southeast Asia unless doing a global comparison.

**Tan et al. (2022), *Acta Geotechnica* (SPH-DEM debris flow)**
Read: SPH-DEM coupling methodology and impact-pressure validation against flume data —
relevant only if you extend into check-dam / retention-structure design.
Skip entirely if staying within flood-only scope.

**Raissi et al. (2019), *Journal of Computational Physics***
Read: the loss-function formulation embedding PDE residuals (early-mid sections) — the
theoretical bridge if you pursue AI+SPH physics integration.
Skip: if PINNs aren't part of your near-term plan; revisit only when you reach that stage.

**Sadegh et al. (2018), *Geophysical Research Letters***
Read: the toolbox usage/workflow section (MvCAT), not just the theory — saves coding time
on copula fitting versus building your `pyvinecopulib` pipeline from scratch.

## Phase 4 — Research frontier (define your contribution)

**Löwe et al. (2021) / Zhu et al. (2021) — surrogate flood models**
Read: architecture + training-data-generation section — precedent for using simulation
output (your SPH data) as training data for a fast surrogate. This is the closest existing
work to your stated strongest-contribution angle.

**AghaKouchak et al. (2020), *Annual Review of Earth & Planetary Sciences***
Read: the data-scarcity / developing-country section — citable framing for the Nepal
data-gap argument in your introduction/motivation.
Skip: sections on hazards outside flood/compound-climate scope.

**Domínguez et al. (2022), *Computer Physics Communications* (DualSPHysics v5)**
Read: new-features list (multi-phase flows, external-solver coupling, GPU performance) —
cross-check against the DualSPHysics version you have installed.

**Nevo et al. (2022), *Nature***
Read: discussion of transfer learning and deployment in data-scarce regions (references
South Asia) — operational framing for your conclusion/future-work section.
Skip: infrastructure/engineering details of Google's serving system — not relevant to your
research contribution.

---

## Reading order logic

Phase 1 gives shared vocabulary and the two core toolchains (copulas, SPH) in parallel.
Phase 2 splits into three tracks (statistics/copula, SPH, AI) that build independently.
Phase 3 pulls each track toward your specific geography (Nepal) and toward the
physics-AI bridge (PINNs) if you choose that extension. Phase 4 is where all three
threads should converge into your actual contribution: **SPH-generated GLOF outflow
hydrographs → LSTM downstream routing → copula joint return period framework**, per
the target-contribution note in `3.KnowledgeBase.md.md`.
