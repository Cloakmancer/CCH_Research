## Next Actions

(Deduplicated and reordered 2026-07-26; the old list repeated steps 1–4 as 5–8.)

1. Generate pseudo-observations: u = R(x)/(n+1) — note the (n+1), not n.
2. Compute Kendall's τ̂ via `scipy.stats.kendalltau`.
3. Estimate θ̂ by MOM: θ̂ = 1/(1 − τ̂) for Gumbel. Fit Gumbel by pseudo-ML on the
   pseudo-observations and cross-check the two estimates — a large gap indicates family
   misspecification, not an optimiser problem.
4. Compare families (Gumbel, Clayton, Frank, Student-t) using: Cramér–von Mises with
   **parametric bootstrap** p-value (asymptotic null is invalid because θ is estimated),
   plus empirical λ_U(u) over u ∈ [0.9, 0.99] overlaid with candidate families. Do not
   select on AIC alone.
5. **Decide the failure mechanism before computing return periods** — peak-alone OR
   volume-alone failure → OR; joint-coincidence failure → AND. T_AND ≥ T_OR always.
6. Compute the chosen return period. Note the result is an *isoline*, not a unique design
   event — resolve via most-likely design event or Kendall return period
   (K_C(t) = t − φ(t)/φ′(t) for Archimedeans).
7. Carry the top 2–3 families through to design values and report the spread rather than
   reporting a single number from a single selected family.

## Update — 2026-08-04

Fit GEV (scipy.stats.genextreme) to annual-maximum daily rainfall for 5 fully-complete stations
(Kathmandu, Pokhara, Biratnagar, Surkhet, Simara Airports/towns; 41 years, 1980–2020) from
`data/Daily Rainfall all nepal.xlsx` (280-station daily series, 65–80% zero days per station).
Note: `Stat_Study/04_Resources/` referenced in `CLAUDE.md` does not exist in the current working
copy — the file was found directly under `data/` instead. Folder map in `CLAUDE.md` is stale on
this point (also says `data/` is empty; it isn't).

**Method decision:** GEV fits block maxima, not the raw daily series — the daily zero-atom does
not reach the fit once annual maxima are extracted (41-year record, no annual max was ever
close to zero). The real decision at this stage was a completeness rule for the extraction step
(a year needs ≥90% of days present to contribute a valid annual max); all 5 stations were 100%
complete so this didn't bind, but it's recorded for the next batch of stations, which won't all
be this clean.

**Results:**

| Station | code | n years | ξ (shape) | μ | σ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kathmandu Airport | 1030 | 41 | −0.003 | 74.07 | 17.29 |
| Pokhara Airport | 804 | 41 | −0.061 | 173.06 | 38.65 |
| Simara Airport | 909 | 41 | −0.101 | 147.37 | 41.11 |
| Biratnagar Airport | 1319 | 41 | −0.185 | 118.33 | 38.66 |
| Surkhet (Birendra Nagar) | 406 | 41 | 0.398 | 96.05 | 25.37 |

**Findings that constrain the next step:**

1. ξ ranges from −0.19 (bounded/Weibull-type upper tail) to +0.40 (heavy Fréchet tail) across
   5 stations in the same country — no single national ξ; the margin has to stay station-specific
   or be pooled deliberately (regional frequency analysis), not averaged.
2. Parametric-bootstrap SE on ξ is large and asymmetric even at n=41: Kathmandu 95% CI
   (−0.25, 0.28); Surkhet 95% CI (0.09, 5.0) — the upper tail is essentially unbounded under
   resampling, driven by one outlier year (423.1 mm vs. next-highest 281.2 mm). This is the
   univariate analogue of the Day 2 finding that a 40-year record can't resolve a tail parameter
   precisely — same problem, one dimension down. MLE is known to be unstable for ξ at this
   record length; **L-moments/PWM should be tried as a more stable alternative before this ξ is
   used downstream**, and the Surkhet outlier year is worth a data-quality check before trusting
   ξ = 0.40.
3. This resolves the Day 1 open thread (fit GEV to Nepal rainfall for ξ) and the 2026-07-25
   "real data vs. pseudo-observations" question in favour of real data — a real margin now
   exists. It does **not** resolve the "need real flood peak-volume dataset" blocker below:
   rainfall AMS is a candidate univariate margin, not the peak/volume pair the copula stage
   needs, which requires a rainfall→discharge step this session didn't touch.

Script: `scripts/fit_gev_annual_maxima.py`.

## Update — 2026-07-29

Day 2 of the statistics track complete as a study unit, same six artefacts as Day 1.

**Standing flag, updated: this is the fourth consecutive session without the copula workflow
moving.** Next Actions 1–7 have not changed since 2026-07-26. That said, Day 2 was not purely
documentation — it produced two things that alter the plan, and they are the reason this session
is worth more than the flag suggests:

1. **λ_U cannot be resolved from one 40-year record.** At u = 0.95 the estimator uses 2 points and
   carries a standard error of ±0.35 against a Gumbel-vs-Gaussian gap of 0.20; at u = 0.99 a
   40-year record usually contains no usable points at all. **Next Action 4's λ_U(u) diagnostic
   therefore cannot be expected to select a family on a single station** — it can only rule things
   out. This promotes **Next Action 7** (carry 2–3 families through to design values and report
   the spread) from a nicety to the load-bearing step, and it is a decision to take before the
   results section is drafted.

2. **A correction to the supplied CCH bridge.** λ_U is invariant to the margins — that is Sklar's
   theorem — so a misspecified marginal cannot move the true λ_U. What it does move is the
   *estimate* (under IFM, which is why semiparametric fitting on ranks is already the default) and
   the design value on the way back out (1.58× across four candidate margins at u = 0.99). The
   conclusion was right, the mechanism needed fixing, and the phrasing would have drawn a
   reviewer's eye.

Next session should open with Next Action 1 and a real margin, not with the site. The Day 1 open
thread — fit a GEV to the Nepal rainfall series for ξ — remains the natural way in, and Day 2 adds
a second, related one: daily rainfall's atom at zero breaks the exact uniformity of U = F(X), so
the ties/zero-inflation treatment has to be chosen and recorded before pseudo-observations are
generated from that series.
